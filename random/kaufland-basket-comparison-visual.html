Methodology — read before the numbers

Kaufland only runs a public, priced online catalogue in Germany (kaufland.de) and Slovakia/Poland (marketplace, shipped goods — not representative of in-store shelf prices). For most markets Kaufland does not publish a browsable shelf-price list, so item-by-item matching across all 7 countries isn't possible from public sources.

  • Germany — priced directly from real, crowdsourced Kaufland shelf prices (supermarktcheck.de product-price database, cross-checked against kaufland.de), for standard/private-label (K-Classic) versions of each item. Basket total: €56.22.
  • Czechia, Slovakia, Romania — the German basket is rescaled using Eurostat's official 2025 Price Level Index for "food & non-alcoholic beverages" (EU=100), which is confirmed for these three countries in Eurostat's June 2026 release.
  • Poland, Croatia, Bulgaria — Eurostat's country-level index wasn't retrievable as text (published only as a table image), so these three use an indicative index cross-referenced from Numbeo-based grocery cost comparisons, sense-checked against Eurostat's qualitative rankings. Treat these three as directional, not exact.

This means: absolute basket costs for DE/CZ/SK/RO rest on official price-level data; PL/HR/BG are best-effort estimates. All are anchored to real, sourced Kaufland Germany prices — not a guess pulled from thin air.

Affordability burden

One week's basket, as % of monthly family income

Romania has the cheapest basket in cash terms among the euro-converted figures except Poland/Bulgaria — but the lowest income too. One week of groceries eats almost 2% of a Romanian family's entire monthly take-home pay, nearly double Germany's burden, even though the German basket costs 28% more in euros.

Two different answers to "priciest"

By sticker price: Germany is priciest (€56.22/week), Poland cheapest (€39.03/week) — a 44% spread.

By what it costs a family relatively: Romania is the toughest place to fill this basket (1.99% of monthly income), Germany the easiest (1.06%) — despite Germany's basket costing the most in absolute euros.

The gap exists because food-price levels converge across the EU far faster than wages do: Germany's food prices are only ~28% above Romania's, but German net income is roughly 2.4x higher.
